Currently, in our v2/NARRATION.md, we have briefly mentioned that aggregation reuses
mechanism/steps like `StampLift` and `StampMerge` already introduced, by first lifting
the anchors of contributing bundle to the same anchor within the same spending epoch,
then use `StampMerge` to merge them.

This description is too short and we sort of downplay its importance. We want to dedicate
non-trivial portion of the chapter on how the aggregation work with concrete back-of-envelop
estimation of the proving/aggregation latency and final amortized cost.

We should at least walk through the aggregation life cycle specified in
https://github.com/tachyon-zcash/zips/blob/b373a6b0ed627904dffbf9289745aa231ddee1eb/zips/draft-tachyon-aggregation-protocol.md

After a broad stroke intro of aggregation lifecycle, we have two options to zoom in on.
One is discuss like the ZIP writeup, on the details of transaction identification and what
happens at the p2p relay level: how to identify an aggregate and its contributing tachygrams,
how to verify an aggregate and relay them forward, how to avoid/substract conjunction of
two overlapping sets etc.
The other is discuss the concrete cost (size and delay) to picture the scaling it brings.
I personally lean towards the latter but you can evaluate and tell me which route fits better
with the rest of the narrative.

For the second option, here's a rough breakdown. We make simplifying assumption that every
wallet just send a standalone bundle (i.e. autonome), and the only aggregator is the
miner/validator who assemble it into a block that he proposes. We completely blackbox or
drop the complex interaction of collaborative aggregation through mempool. This simplification
is highly reasonable because in practice miners, who benefit from squeezing in as many
transactions into a block as possible to reap more fees within the block limit has the
strongest incentive to aggregate.
First, let's look at latency.
The miner/aggregator first need to lift all contributing stamps to the same anchor,
this is a single steps for each member stamp, but this is parallelizable, the overall latency,
assuming infinite cores, is just one PCD step: `StampLift`. Then we have to pair-wise
`StampMerge`, which forms a 2-arity tree, with log depth, thus log number of PCD steps.
Given a PCD step time of 1.2 sec, measured locally on a consumer laptop with the multicore
features on (the earlier unoptimized figure in https://github.com/tachyon-zcash/ragu/pull/873
was ~2 sec), and a 25 sec block time after NU7, reserving 2 sec for final transaction
transmission, and another 2 sec for verification and conservatively other Zakura node logic,
we are left with roughly 20 sec for aggregation. That is 20 / 1.2 ~= 16 sequential steps:
1 for the lift, so 15 levels of the merge tree. With unlimited cores we can aggregate
2^15 = 32,768 transactions (one stamp each) per block, which supports 32,768 / 25 sec
~= 1.3K TPS even if miner has no help and is the only aggregator for the block.
Second, let's look at size.
Given the compressed size of 7.4KB reported in https://github.com/tachyon-zcash/ragu/pull/462,
assuming each tx is 2-in-2-out, thus 8 tachygrams,
and 8 * 32 = 256 bytes in tg, 4 * 2 * 32 = 256 for (cv, rk) spend/output actions,
assuming there's no ML-KEM first-contact ciphertext which is ~2KB each (will dominate block space thus clearly need an upgrade to `MAX_BLOCK_BYTES: u64 = 2_000_000;`); we only assume
symmetric AEAD ciphertext of another 256 bytes per tx. 
This gives us (very roughly, since we ignore some other smaller-sized fields):
(2MB - 7.4KB) / (3*256) = 2.6K transaction per block.
clearly size is lower ceiling at this moment. but assuming we can safely assume that this
size limit would increase with our TPS, and a 20MB block is still reasonable which gives
us 26K tachyon tx per block, and that's roughly 1K TPS given the 25 second block time.
This still fits the latency budget: 26K needs 1 lift + 15 merge levels = 16 steps
= 16 * 1.2 = 19.2 sec <= 20 sec (and the 2MB block, 2.6K tx, needs only 1 + 12 = 13 steps
= 15.6 sec). So proving latency allows ~1.3K TPS and size allows ~1K TPS at 20MB: size is
the binding ceiling.

Now the estimation above is first-order and too long, our presentation should never be
as pedantic and dull. We should highlight the conclusion with convincingly enough details,
you will be the judge.
